# The openings

Generated by `node tools/apertures.mjs`. **Do not edit by hand.**

> *"i think i need to think about it in terms of intersections, where are the
> openings, how big are they, etc."*

Measured across every wall the grammar can present — all
427,020 storey-faces of 1,048 distinct walls. The answer is much smaller
than the block count suggests.

## The vertical is already solved

A block is three plans extruded, so **every opening in the grammar is a
rectangle whose height is 3, 6 or 9 yards and whose sill sits at 0, 3 or 6.**
There is no design freedom in the vertical at all — an opening occupies one
storey, two, or all three.

| | 3 yd | 6 yd | 9 yd | other |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **height** | 62.5% | 23.1% | 14.3% | 0.1% |
| **sill at** | 23.8% | 25.1% | — | 51.0% at 0 |

The "other" in both rows is the arches, and only the arches — the vault curve
is the one thing in the game that does not land on a storey.

## So the only real variable is the WORD

One storey of one wall, nine yards across. Of the **512** possible nine-bit
words the whole grammar uses **13**, and three of those are vestigial:
they occur only in the 58 arches and account for
400 storey-faces out of 427,020.

```
  a wall  =  three storeys, each one of TEN words
```

1,030 of the 2,197 possible word-triples actually occur, so in practice you may
pick a word per storey and the block usually exists.

## The ten words

Drawn across the nine yards of the face, `#` stone and `.` opening. Two blocks
meet flush when their facing walls are **identical word for word** — not
mirrored. Rotation is free at placement, which is why the handed pairs below
carry exactly equal counts: a block offering `return-l` can be turned to offer
`return-r`.

### Three plans are the sole source of their word

That makes them structurally fragile: a storey built from one can only ever
meet a storey built from the same one.

- **`ell-deep`** is the only maker of `jamb-l`
- **`ell-deep`** is the only maker of `jamb-r`
- **`bar-wide`** is the only maker of `island`
- **`rounded`** is the only maker of `chamfer`


**And it is expensive.** Grouping every block by whether it presents a
sole-source word on any wall, and measuring how many of the 10,826 can meet
its walls:

| presents | blocks | mean reach | |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| nothing sole-source | 5,471 | 239 | the baseline |
| a word only `ell-deep` makes | 2,667 | 204 | **1.2× worse** |
| a word only `bar-wide` makes | 1,696 | 126 | **1.9× worse** |
| a word only `rounded` makes | 992 | 29 | **8.1× worse** |

So a block showing `chamfer` anywhere can meet **29** of the 10,826 instead of
239. Nearly half the grammar carries one of these words on some wall. **A
second plan emitting `chamfer` would multiply the reach of 992 blocks by
eight** — and it need not resemble `rounded` at all, it only has to leave
stone in the same nine yards.

This is the mechanism behind the census's one dead end. `S:rounded,rounded,
rounded` presents `chamfer` on all three storeys of all four walls, `rounded`
is the only plan that makes `chamfer`, and `rounded` is four-fold symmetric so
there is exactly one such block. **It can only ever meet itself, and there is
no second one.** Not a quirk of that block — a property of the word.

## How big are the openings

One sub-block is 1 yard; a block is 9 yards across.

| width | share | | openings on a wall | share |
|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 3 yd | 46.0% | | 1 | 49.0% |
| 9 yd | 28.1% | | 2 | 31.1% |
| 4 yd | 13.3% | | 3 | 15.0% |
| 2 yd | 5.4% | | 0 | 2.7% |
| 1 yd | 4.4% | | 4 | 1.7% |
| 6 yd | 2.8% | | 5 | 0.4% |

**Three yards is the doorway** — 46% of every opening in the game — and nine
yards, the whole face, is next at 28%. Between them they are three quarters of
all openings, which is why the vocabulary coheres at all.

|---|---:|---|

| loggia | 36.7% | open to the ceiling |

| window | 12.3% | masonry above and below |
