# The openings

Generated by `node tools/apertures.mjs`. **Do not edit by hand.**

> *"i think i need to think about it in terms of intersections, where are the
> openings, how big are they, etc."*

Measured across every wall the grammar can present — all
3,030,924 storey-faces of 2,817 distinct walls. The answer is much smaller
than the block count suggests.

## The vertical is already solved

A block is three plans extruded, so **every opening in the grammar is a
rectangle whose height is 3, 6 or 9 yards and whose sill sits at 0, 3 or 6.**
There is no design freedom in the vertical at all — an opening occupies one
storey, two, or all three.

| | 3 yd | 6 yd | 9 yd | other |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **height** | 52.0% | 24.2% | 23.8% | 0.0% |
| **sill at** | 20.6% | 21.7% | — | 57.6% at 0 |

The "other" in both rows is the arches, and only the arches — the vault curve
is the one thing in the game that does not land on a storey.

## So the only real variable is the WORD

One storey of one wall, nine yards across. Of the **512** possible nine-bit
words the whole grammar uses **17**, and three of those are vestigial:
they occur only in the 104 arches and account for
756 storey-faces out of 3,030,924.

```
  a wall  =  three storeys, each one of TEN words
```

2,786 of the 2,197 possible word-triples actually occur, so in practice you may
pick a word per storey and the block usually exists.

### Three plans are the sole source of their word

That makes them structurally fragile: a storey built from one can only ever
meet a storey built from the same one.

- **`ell-deep`** is the only maker of `jamb-l`
- **`ell-deep`** is the only maker of `jamb-r`
- **`bar-wide`** is the only maker of `island`


**IT USED TO BE EXPENSIVE, AND AT R = 2 IT IS NOT.** Grouping every block by
whether it presents a sole-source word on any wall, and measuring how much of
the grammar can meet its walls:

| presents | blocks | mean reach | |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| nothing sole-source | 49,814 | 852 | the baseline |
| a word only `ell-deep` makes | 11,070 | 802 | **1.1× worse** |
| a word only `bar-wide` makes | 6,036 | 610 | **1.4× worse** |

**THIS WAS THE MECHANISM BEHIND THE GRAMMAR'S ONE DEAD END, AND THE OWNER
CLOSED IT BY CHANGING THE RADIUS.** At R = 2.5 the worst of these words was
`chamfer`: `S:rounded,rounded,rounded` presented it on all three storeys of
all four walls, `rounded` was the only plan that made it, and `rounded` is
four-fold symmetric — so there was exactly one such block, and it could only
ever meet itself. A block showing `chamfer` anywhere reached **29** against a
baseline of 239: eight times worse.

At R = 2 the round bites less than a whole sub-block out of the corner, so
`rounded` speaks `wall` like everything else, `chamfer` is not in the
vocabulary at all, and **nothing in the grammar meets nothing**. Read the
table above rather than this paragraph: the sole-source words that survive
cost close to nothing, and their WORST case is better than the general worst
case. Something else now sets the floor on how placeable a block is, and this
document does not yet say what. See BACKLOG 0q.

**Three yards is the doorway** — 46% of every opening in the game — and nine
yards, the whole face, is next at 28%. Between them they are three quarters of
all openings, which is why the vocabulary coheres at all.
