# Handoff — Piranesi, 2026-08-06

**Read this before touching anything.** The project was redirected mid-build; a
lot of the code in the tree is dead, and the live URL is the old game. This
document is the keystone: what the game is, the laws that must not be
"improved", the traps already paid for, and what to do next.

---

## 0. What happened, in one paragraph

The first build (`carceri`) was a free-flight 3-D architectural builder rendered
as a copperplate etching. The renderer was good and the owner liked it — "you
did capture something of the atmosphere" — but it was the wrong game. He wanted
**a peaceful, layer-by-layer grid builder** whose pieces are **over-the-top
Piranesi blocks**. His verdict: *"its best to start over and just take the
lessons you learned from it."* So: repo and directory renamed to `piranesi`,
game rewritten, renderer kept.

---

## 1. What the game IS

A peaceful builder. **No fail state, no score, no economy, nothing ever taken.**

**Two modes.**

| | Build | Explore |
|---|---|---|
| camera | rotatable **three-quarter overhead** | inside the space, moving through it |
| pitch | yes, ~30–40° | **zero, always** |
| purpose | a view of the **model** | a view of the **space** |
| the picture | a drawing board | a **plate** |

**Layers.** You place on one flat horizontal layer at a time.
Scroll / PageUp-PageDown changes it.

- current layer — **full opacity**
- layers above — **ghosted**
- layers below — **an artificial shadow**

In the owner's words, this "will give an idea of whats going on in other layers
without confusing the player about what is happening on the exact layer they are
working."

**Blocks.** The owner's framing, which is the design:

> the blocks, just like real toy blocks, are a metaphor for modular
> construction. like functions in programming they represent small pieces you can
> use to assemble larger works. because its giovanni battista, the blocks are
> over the top, imposing, and not necessarily practical. because of the
> labyrinthian nature of his work a cubic block might have a tower, a staircase
> and an archway all on the same block.
>
> the finished output of this game might make an MC Escher like structure where
> unsupported stairways snake through labyrinthian structures.

And the key implementation idea, also his:

> to create that unnatural block feel i recommend breaking the main blocks up.
> the main blocks can be procedurally generated by smaller sub blocks … the
> player only interacts with the main blocks.

**Subject:** one vast interior. **Reference:** antique chromolithograph wooden
toy blocks (the boxed sets with printed faces) crossed with the Carceri plates.

## 2. The architecture as it stands

```
js/parts.js      SUB-BLOCKS. pier, archway, stair, catwalk, balustrade, drum,
                 turret, gantry, beam, chain, ring, lamp, grating, corbel.
                 Sub-cell units. The player never touches these.       [NEW]
js/compose.js    THE COMPOSER. 6 archetypes + 3–5 seeded attachments →
                 one main block. The socket ladder. buildCatalog().    [NEW]
js/mesh.js       geometry primitives + the per-face (u,v) frame        [KEEP]
js/world.js      the lattice: multi-cell footprints, two tiers,
                 coincidence culling                                   [KEEP]
js/math.js       axes, BOTH cameras, deterministic hashes              [KEEP]
js/ink.js        the plate: transmittance, strokes, paper, develop     [KEEP]
js/engrave.js    the engraver: stencil, lines, hatching, coursing      [KEEP]

js/blocks.js     the old fine catalogue                                [DEAD*]
js/modules.js    the old 6-cell cube system                            [DEAD]
js/scenes.js     the old scenes                                        [DEAD]
js/main.js       the old game (free-flight, no layers)                 [DEAD]
```

\* `blocks.js` is dead as a *player-facing catalogue*, but `parts.js` was written
from it and some geometry is worth stealing before it is deleted. Do not delete
these four until the new game runs; they are the only working reference for how
`World` and `Engraver` are driven.

**Scale.** `SUB = 8` sub-cells per main block, `1.5 m` per sub-cell ⇒ a **12 m
block**, and a man is a little over one sub-cell tall. (The old build used
2 m cells and 6-cell cubes for the same 12 m; the sub-cell got finer because a
block now has to hold a staircase *inside* it.)

## 3. THE LAWS

Each of these is load-bearing. Several are pinned in `test/laws.test.mjs`.

### 3.1 The socket ladder

**Four deck levels — sub-z 0, 2, 4, 6 — are the ONLY heights at which anything
may cross a block boundary.**

This is the single rule that makes procedurally generated blocks assemble. A
stair leaving the `+x` face at level 2 meets whatever the neighbour put at level
2, *because there is nowhere else for either of them to be*. Constrain the joins
absolutely and let the middle of a block be as mad as it likes.

It is also where the Escher comes from: two blocks each with a stair at level 2
make a continuous run, neither of them knew about the other, and nothing checked
whether the result could stand up.

**If you widen this ladder the builder becomes a bag of clutter.**

### 3.2 The camera never pitches — *in explore mode*

Look at any Carceri plate: the piers are dead vertical on the paper, all of
them, all the way up, while the arches race to vanishing points left and right.
That is a vertical picture plane and a **rising front** (`shift`), not a tilt.
Four lines of algebra in `math.js` prove verticals stay vertical iff the
camera's forward and right vectors have no z component.

**Build mode is allowed to pitch** and is honest about being a different
instrument — a view of the model, not of the space. No rising front can ever
show you the top of a floor slab. At pitch 0 the z terms are exactly zero and
the vertical-line test still passes.

### 3.3 Ink is transmittance, not coverage

Two layers of ink pass `T₁·T₂`, so strokes **multiply**. That one choice gives
correct cross-hatching free: darker everywhere, much darker at crossings,
saturating toward black instead of clipping. Additive coverage puts dark knots
at every crossing; `max` makes cross-hatching do nothing at all.

### 3.4 Faces cancel by coincidence, not by a solidity mask

Two faces occupying the same place and pointing opposite ways are both
invisible; hash the rounded vertex ring. A voxel-style "which sides are full"
declaration cannot express a wall with an arch through it. **This is an
aesthetic feature, not an optimisation** — it is what turns a row of bays into a
receding tunnel.

Blocks tag their boundary faces via a **post-pass over the finished mesh**
(`tagBoundaryFaces`), never by threading `side` through every part: a part has
no idea where it was placed.

### 3.5 The measured hatching

Not recalled — **measured**, off high-resolution museum scans (Rijksmuseum, Met,
AIC, LACMA), by structure tensor and rotate-then-autocorrelate, across five
plates and both editions. Four of my confident assumptions were wrong:

| I had built | the plates measure |
|---|---|
| spacing tightens as tone darkens | **pitch CONSTANT** ~0.80 mm; **stroke WIDTH** is the tone knob (duty 0.25→0.86) |
| cross at 54°/−34°, "never 90°" | cross is **SQUARE**, 88–96° off the primary |
| hatch follows each surface's form | **one hand at 40° backslash** holds 53–66% of all line energy |
| bimodal — bare paper vs black | 1st state is **mid-and-dark**: 1% bare, 0% solid, 55% ink |

Reconciliation shipped: **only the first hatch family describes form** (a vault
intrados, a drum flank, timber grain keep their surface frame); every darkening
family takes the 40° hand. Cross a vault square to its own wrap and it reads as
basketwork.

**Refuse low-resolution reference images.** Three web-resolution scans produced
three different confident pitch answers (0.30, 0.37, 0.52 mm) that were purely
their own pixel floors. Require ≥ 4 pixels per hatch period.

### 3.6 Determinism

**No `Math.random` anywhere in the renderer or the composer.** Every wobble is a
hash of a stroke's identity; every block is a hash of its seed. A random-per-
frame wobble makes the plate *boil* as you move, and a random block cannot be
saved.

And: **stroke seeds come from a block's ADDRESS, never its index.** Face ids are
a counter over the block map, so inserting one block renumbers everything after
it — and if the hand were keyed to that, laying one paving slab would redraw the
handwriting of the entire building. Test-pinned.

## 4. Traps already paid for

Each of these cost a plate to find. They are listed with the **symptom**,
because that is what you will see when you break one again.

| symptom | cause |
|---|---|
| floors and walls come back as **graph paper** | cancelled faces were still emitting crease edges. An arris is not there if the face is not there. |
| **paved ground in daylight renders black** | the sky-visibility rays hit the face's *own* block. A visibility ray must always be told what not to count. |
| **handrails floating in mid-air over nothing** | a railing placed on a stair *deleted* the stair. One block per cell cannot express a fitting. → two tiers. |
| **broad pale bands radiating from the vanishing point**, reading completely plausibly as shafts of light | fixed-stride ray marching through a lattice aliases at cell corners. Use an exact voxel DDA. *Nearly shipped as a feature.* |
| **a pale gutter across the stonework at every block boundary** | the ragged stroke ends were a *percentage* of stroke length: 2 px on a small face, a finger's width on a long wall. Raggedness is an **absolute** amount. |
| **the whole plate uniformly 71% near-black** with a correctly calibrated hatcher | single-bounce visibility says a 50 m vaulted hall is black. True of the geometry, false of every interior ever drawn. Needs an explicit bounce floor. |
| a vault reads as **basketwork** | its cross-hatch was square to its own wrap. Only family 0 describes form. |
| a sliced curved form reads as a **paling fence** | fresh vertices per column. The edge table keys on vertex *index*; occupying the same edge is not sharing it. |
| a **solid black lid across the open middle** of a vault at a tile seam | a face group emitted where the tile holds no material. |
| a curve becomes a **horizontal shelf** where it leaves a tile | the arc was clamped to the window. Put the window-crossings *on* column boundaries so nothing needs clamping. |
| **a small beige square on a black background** | the canvas sized itself from a layout that had not settled → 28×320. **Use a `ResizeObserver` on the stage, never a boot-time `getBoundingClientRect`.** |

**The pattern across all of them:** every one was invisible in code review and
obvious in a picture — and two of them (the light shafts, the tile lid) looked
like deliberate art. **Render it and look at it.**

### The two process mistakes worth not repeating

1. **I saw the 28×320 canvas on my first browser check, worked around it by
   resizing the pane, and moved on.** The owner then hit it as a blank page. A
   symptom you route around is a bug you shipped.
2. **I twice used PowerShell `Get-Content`/`Set-Content` to edit source and
   mangled the file's encoding** (em-dashes → mojibake). Use the editing tools
   for source. PowerShell is for running things.

## 5. Instruments

Build tools to see with. Two blind art passes is the going rate for not having
them.

| tool | what it is for |
|---|---|
| `tools/blockshot.mjs` | **The one this project cannot proceed without.** A contact sheet of generated blocks *with their recipes printed beside them*. One block tells you nothing about whether a generator has variety or is drawing the same block with the furniture moved. `--run N` puts three of the *same* block in a row, because blocks that look fine alone can still refuse to meet. |
| `tools/tonecheck.mjs` | The tone transfer curve. Hatch a flat wall at known tones, measure the ink. **Run after any change to the hatcher, the stroke rasteriser or the register ladder.** |
| `tools/plateshot.mjs` | Pull an impression headless — real catalogue, world, camera, engraver. `--crop`, `--passes`, `--stats`. |
| `tools/png.mjs` | Zero-dependency PNG writer. The reason the renderer never touches a canvas. |
| `tools/serve.mjs`, `tools/snap.mjs` | Static server; receives a PNG POSTed from the running page. |

`tonecheck` earned its place on its first run: it found the hatcher was
**non-monotonic** — tone 0.50 drew *lighter* than 0.44, in the mid-range where
every wall lives — and **nothing in any picture said so.** It just looked like a
decision somebody had made.

**An instrument must be able to refuse.** State the number it saw, not just
pass/fail: the "blank sheet" assertion was rewritten to print the mean ink
because otherwise you have to rebuild it to learn how close the miss was.

## 8. ~~THE OPEN QUESTION~~ — ANSWERED 2026-08-06, and it moved the whole palette

I asked what "ghosted" means in a medium with no alpha, and offered the etcher's
answer (outline only, a preliminary state of the plate). The owner answered
something better, and wider than the question:

> "i dont expect the finished work output to look like an etching. i figure the
> game will be fairly muted and neutral in its pallet. mostly browns and greys
> using warm and cool neutrals to define space. if the full tonal range went
> from 0-100 with 0 being pure white and 100 being black, the values of the
> ghost layers would be from 0-30 and the dark layers would be base value +30
> to 40."

**Neither of my two readings.** Ghosting is a **value range**, not a drawing
mode. That is a better answer than the one I was arguing for, because a value
range is continuous — a ghost still models, it is just quiet — where
outline-only throws away everything the layer above could have told you about
its mass.

Three things follow, and they are now built:

1. **The bands are a tone transform applied BEFORE the hatcher**, not a
   composite after it. A ghosted face asks for a third of its tone and the
   hatcher draws a third of the line. Ghosting is therefore also most of the
   cost of a layer *given back* — which matters, because re-render time was a
   real complaint about the first build. `js/palette.js`, `bandTone`.
2. **The output is no longer trying to be an impression.** The ground went from
   cream laid paper to a light neutral, and the single warm near-black became
   two — one warm brown, one cool grey — mixed per pixel by the surface under
   it. `faceWarmth` leads with the face's ORIENTATION and only then with the
   light. Measured spread on a real model: red-minus-blue from −11 to +28.
3. **The working layer is drawn with headroom** (`LIVE_HEAD = 0.62`). Mine, not
   the owner's, and forced by measurement: "base + 30" needs base to have thirty
   to give, and it did not. See §10.

The owner also offered to start with a single layer and add the stack later.
Not taken: the bands turned out to be a transform rather than a subsystem, so
the stack cost almost nothing on top of the flat case, and the layer view is the
part of the game he could not see at all before.

## 10. Session two — the layer view, and four things measurement caught

Everything in §7's list from "the layer view" down to "delete the dead files" is
done. `js/game.js` and a rewritten `index.html` replace `js/main.js`, which is
gone. `js/build.js` holds the drawing board — layers, the two cameras, the
cursor's ground intersection. 24 tests, all passing.

**The `ResizeObserver` is in** (`game.js`, top). That was the bug that made the
owner see a small beige square on a black background, and the file says so at
the point where it is fixed.

### The instrument that made the rest of this possible

`tools/layershot.mjs`. It builds a stepped three-layer stack, renders the build
view, and prints **what each band ASKED the hatcher for beside what it actually
DELIVERED**, in the owner's 0–100 units. Everything below was found by that
table and none of it by looking at a picture.

| symptom in the table | what it was |
|---|---|
| ghost band reporting **26** when it should be ≤30, but live was 29 — bands "not separating" | **the instrument was lying.** The ghost pass draws over the solid one, so a ghost in front of a wall was measuring the wall. Score a ghost pixel only where the solid stencil is empty. |
| `asked NaN` for two whole bands | **`faceTone` could return NaN.** Iron carries a +0.28 material bias on top of a value already near 1 on an unlit face → `t > 1` → `1 − t` negative → `Math.pow(negative, 1.45)`. NaN fails every comparison, so it sailed through the hatcher's own "too light to draw" guard and came out as strokes of NaN width. **The ironwork on the darkest faces was simply missing and nothing said so.** Clamp before the curve. |
| below band asked for **60** and delivered **38** | **the build camera broke aerial perspective.** It stands 420 cells back with a long lens so the view is near-orthographic — which put the whole model past `fogDepth`, so every face was drawn at maximum haze: thinnest needle, fewest hatch families, tone knocked back 22%. A band asking for value 60 was being drawn with the equipment for value 20. Fog is now measured **from the nearest face drawn**, not from the eye. Depth in a picture is depth *through the subject*; how far the draughtsman stands back is not part of it. |
| live 47 vs below 60 — only 13 points apart where the arithmetic promised 27 | **line work sets a floor neither band's tone could climb out of.** Both bands carried identical silhouettes and creases. Layers below now get a fatter needle as well as a lifted tone (`SHADOW_LINE`). Weight above 1 goes into the *width*, never the strength — strength is an opacity, and over 1 the transmittance multiply goes negative and the pixel comes back brighter than paper. |

Two more, from the test suite rather than the instrument:

- A face at base 90 ghosted out at **31**, one point outside the spec, because
  the ghost band was the tone scaled *and then* lifted off a floor. It now spans
  `[floor, ceiling]`.
- **`requestAnimationFrame` does not fire in a hidden tab, and the browser
  preview runs hidden.** A test that changed the layer, waited, and read the
  stats got the stale numbers from whenever the tab was last in front —
  identical for every layer, which reads exactly like a broken layer switch. I
  lost a round to that. `bite(quality)` is now callable synchronously and
  exposed on `window.piranesi`. **Anything that has to be verified must be
  callable, not only schedulable.**

### What the numbers are now

On a fourteen-block, three-layer stack, working on layer 1:

```
                     asked   got
        +1 up          8      23      (spec: 0–30)
         live         26      47
       1 down         53      61
```

Delivery still runs above the request in the solid bands, and that is line ink,
not hatcher error — small faces at build scale carry a lot of edge per unit
area. The separation is real and legible; it is not yet generous.

### Still open, and now better understood

- **Composed blocks never cancel a face against each other.** `cancelled` is 0
  for any run of composed blocks, where a sliced module reports 428. The
  boundary tagging works — 5107 of 16648 catalogue faces carry a `side` — but
  two neighbouring blocks only cancel if their boundary faces occupy the *same
  place*, and procedurally different masses do not. So a row of blocks is a row
  of separate boxes, not one continuous interior, and coincidence culling — the
  thing that turns a colonnade into a tunnel — never fires. **This is the single
  biggest obstacle to "one vast interior" and it is a block-design problem, not
  a renderer one.** The socket ladder constrains where things may *cross* a
  boundary; nothing yet makes the boundary itself a shared surface.
- The build view has no enclosure: the model sits in open air, so almost every
  face sees the whole sky and the tonal range starts low. An interior wants a
  shell.
- Block masses are still plain boxes wanting relief, wear and ornament, and six
  archetypes still want to be ten.

## 11. The skin — the engraving is not the game's look

Said plainly, because I got it wrong twice:

> "as much as i like that texture shader and the vibe this has i dont think its
> quite right for this game. lets try a middle grey with a stone texture. lines
> just outline the blocks."
>
> "you might want to save the shader for another project. it has a fun 'take on
> me' aesthetic."

**There are now two skins, and they are two different pictures, not a setting on
one picture.** `DEFAULTS.skin`:

- **`'stone'` — the game.** A printed middle grey with a solid stone texture
  through it. **Zero hatch strokes anywhere.** The line work steps back to being
  a contour, which is what an antique chromolithograph toy block does.
- **`'hatch'` — the software line engraver.** No fills anywhere; every value on
  the sheet made of bitten line. Kept whole, kept working, kept under test.

### Why the engraver stays in the tree

The owner wants it for another project. **Do not delete it, do not let it rot,
and do not "simplify" it away when tidying** — it is the more interesting
renderer of the two and it is a portable asset. It is self-contained in
`js/engrave.js` (the `hatch`, `course`, `flags`, `grain` methods and the `LAYERS`
/ duty-cycle machinery) plus `js/ink.js`, and it is calibrated against measured
museum scans. `tonecheck.mjs` is its instrument and CI runs it. Anything that
breaks it should fail `the stone skin draws no hatching at all, and the engraver
still can`.

His name for it — "a fun *Take On Me* aesthetic" — is the right one: it is a
rotoscope-line look, and it belongs on something that wants to be a drawing all
the way down.

### How the stone skin works

`js/stone.js` + `Engraver.stoneFill` + a second buffer on `Plate`.

**The texture is 3-D, in world space, not a per-face UV.** A block's faces are
cut through one lump of rock: evaluate the texture at the world point a pixel
actually stands on and the mottling runs round an arris without a seam, a
stair's tread and its riser belong to the same stone, and two neighbouring
blocks are two pieces of one quarry. Done as **one sweep of the finished
stencil** — the stencil already knows which face is under each pixel and the
projection inverts in six multiplies, so every pixel can ask the world where it
stands.

**It is band-limited by hand.** The build camera is near-orthographic from 420
cells back and the explore camera is inside the room, so the same texture is
seen at wildly different scales in one session. An octave finer than a pixel
does not add detail — it adds *crawling* noise when the camera moves, which
reads as a broken shader. Each octave fades out over the last doubling before
it reaches the pixel grid. Pinned by a test.

**Middle grey is a remap, not a constant** (`stoneRange`, `STONE_LOW 0.24`,
`STONE_HIGH 0.74`). The engraver's tone curve is built for bare paper against
near-solid black; handed straight to a fill it makes the model read as painted
card. The stone skin compresses the whole range so nothing is paper and nothing
is black — which is also what leaves the layer bands room to be the loudest
signal on the screen.

**The fill multiplies, it does not assign.** The ghost pass runs over the same
buffer, and a ghost is a haze laid *over* what is behind it; assigning would let
a pale layer above lighten the solid beneath, which is the one thing ghosting
must never do.

### The cost, measured honestly

| 932×756, 14 blocks | stone | hatch |
|---|---|---|
| build view | 232 ms | 213 ms |
| explore view | **160 ms** | 215 ms |

The stone skin is *cheaper* than the engraver in explore and level with it in
build. Two traps on the way there, both in the per-pixel loop:

- A readable `c(dx,dy,dz)` closure inside the noise was **one allocation per
  octave per pixel** — three and a half million a frame. It turned a 250 ms
  plate into a 1000 ms one. This is the only hot loop in the project that runs
  per pixel rather than per face; it is worth being ugly exactly here.
- **Do not time this in the browser preview.** The pane runs the tab hidden and
  throttles it unevenly — it reported stone at 810 ms against hatch at 410 ms,
  a 2× penalty that does not exist. Node is the honest clock. Same root cause as
  the `requestAnimationFrame` trap in §10.

## 12. The cube law, and the two things it fixed on the way

The owner drew the modular rules on graph paper and then settled the unit
directly: **"its actually 9x9 blocks if you want to get technical."**

```
sub-block   3 ft   = 0.9144 m             the lattice unit
main block  9x9x9  = 27 ft = 8.2296 m     a man is 0.21 of it
R           2.5    = 7.5 ft               everything curved
R_WHOLE     4.5    = 13.5 ft, == SUB/2    the inscribed circle
planes      0, 2, 2.5, 3, 4.5, 6, 6.5, 7, 9
```

`R_WHOLE === SUB / 2` is the mechanism, not a coincidence: an arch struck with
it springs and crowns **on** the boundary planes, so two neighbours agree at the
seam by construction. Test-pinned.

I first built this at 3x3x3 with the radii in feet, which fits the drawing
equally well — the ratios are identical and only the unit differs. His reading
is better and it is not close: at 3x3x3 the whole-block circle spans nine feet,
which is a doorway, and he said that form is "mostly for making high vaulted
arches". At 9x9x9 it spans twenty-seven. **The rescale cost four constants**,
because every form is written in terms of `SUB`, `R` and `R_WHOLE` — that is
what parameterising against the spec buys.

### What it fixed

**Composed blocks never cancelled a face against each other — 0, where a sliced
module got 428.** Top of the backlog, and the biggest obstacle to "one vast
interior". Now a run of four cancels 6 to 38 faces depending on the form, and
five vault blocks in a row read as one continuous barrel with no seam anywhere.
His "blocks that are just solid space, so there will be situations where one
block abuts another" is what does it.

### And a bug the new forms exposed

**Every block claimed all 27 cells for the light.** `World.place` stamps the
whole footprint and the light marches that same map, so `bore-y` — a tunnel you
can see straight through — was as opaque as a wall, an arcade let no light
through its arches, and the thing the lattice march exists for (a tunnel getting
darker the further in you look, which no normal-based model can produce) was
being thrown away one block at a time. It never read as a fault because the old
composer's blocks were *also* mostly void, so everything was uniformly too dark
and it looked like a mood.

`js/solidity.js` splits the two questions: the footprint still **reserves** the
whole box, and a separate mask says where there is **stone**. Ray cast per cell,
once per catalogue entry, cached. After: vault 6 of 27, bore 12, column 15,
solid 27.

Three things in it are load-bearing and two were faults first:

- **Do not sample the cell centre.** Cells are one sub-block, so a centre lands
  on a half — and 4.5 is the block's own mid-plane, where every arc is struck
  from, where a vault springs, and where the whole-block circle is tangent to
  the walls. A bore came back solid down its own axis. Sixty-four samples at
  quarters-offset-by-an-eighth instead; pinned against 0, 0.5 and 1.
- **The 20% threshold is doing real work.** It is the line between a thin wall
  crossing a cell, which must block light, and the flank of a column clipping
  the corner of one, which must not. Eight coarser samples could not separate
  them at any threshold — the wall fell between the sample planes.
- **Without the bounding-box prefilter it is unshippable.** 313 ms for one old
  8x8x8 block, seven and a half seconds to warm a catalogue, on the main thread,
  at boot. The ray runs very nearly along +x, so a face whose y or z range it
  never enters needs no plane solve. 5.7x.
