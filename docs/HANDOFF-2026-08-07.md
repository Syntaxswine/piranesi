# Handoff — Piranesi, 2026-08-07

**Read this first.** `HANDOFF-2026-08-06.md` is still the keystone for what the
game *is*, the renderer, the palette and the cube law — none of that changed.
This document covers the day the grammar was **walked, measured, ranked and then
rebuilt to the owner's own specification**, and it supersedes that document's
§13–§15 only in the sense of continuing them.

## 0. What happened, in one paragraph

`buildCatalog` used to deal twenty-four blocks out of a space nobody had ever
looked at. In one session that space was enumerated end to end, every block in
it measured, a hundred-block kit chosen from it and proved to tile, the whole
interface re-described as **openings** rather than as hash keys, and then the
owner supplied his own archetype spec — coded in X and O — which found a gap
none of the measurement had: **the vocabulary had no single wall.** Six plans
closed most of it and two more closed the rest. The grammar went from 10,826
blocks to **66,920**, and from **6 of his 13 archetypes to 13 of 14**.

## 2. THE LAWS learned this session

### 2.1 A number that agrees with everything is not a measurement

This happened **four separate times** in one afternoon, and every instance was
confident, plausible and useless.

| measured | said | the fault |
|---|---|---|
| `flush` | 4/4 for **99.8%** | not a bug — a RESULT. The cube law works so well that "does it join" cannot separate blocks. The question had to move to *how common* a wall is. |
| `support` | **100% for all 10,826** | averaged eight z-interfaces, six of which are interior to a layer and cannot fail. Measured only where a slice hands over: 0–100%, and a third of the grammar fails it. |
| the composite | **82 tied at 1.000** | a "top forty" that was an alphabetical listing inside one enormous tie. A ranking that does not rank looks exactly like one that does. |
| `ways` | best blocks all 3/3 | satisfied by **walking over the roof**. |

**Check the distribution before believing a ranking.** `new Set(scores).size` and
"how many tie at the top" are two lines, and there is now a self-check that fails
the build when the score stops discriminating.

### 2.2 Connectivity is global; a local objective is blind to it

A kit of 100 came back **99 of 100 blocks flush on all four walls — and it was
two kits**: an 87-block body and a 13-block island of arches with no way between
them. Every local measurement said excellent.

The greedy pass cannot see it because it rewards a block for answering walls
*nothing else answers* — and the arches went in consecutively and **answer each
other**, so no arch wall was ever unanswered and nothing was drawn toward them.
It needs its own pass (`repairConnectivity`), and so does the vertical
(`repairVertical`): **17 of 100 blocks had nothing in the kit to stand on**,
which is what stopped a building having a third storey.

### 2.3 Every piece of a plan must be DISJOINT

Two overlapping rectangles are crossed twice by the solidity ray, so parity
reads the overlap as **void**. An L built from two full-length walls came back
with a hole where they meet, and three of five new archetypes missed on the
first attempt for that reason alone. It is why `bored` is cut into four and
`frame` into four: **the mesh has no notion of a union.**

### 2.4 The vertical has no design freedom

A block is three plans extruded, so **every opening in the grammar is a
rectangle 3, 6 or 9 yards tall with its sill at 0, 3 or 6.** Nothing else
occurs except in the arches — the vault curve is the one thing in the game that
does not land on a storey. So the only real variable is the WORD: one storey of
one wall, nine yards across, and there are **twelve of them**.

### 2.5 A derived quantity written down by hand is a bug with a delay on it

Twice, from the same cause. `ell-deep` declared `mass: 0.56` for geometry
covering 0.889 — the number recorded the *intent* and the code did the opposite,
and `rollRecipe` sorts heaviest-first. And `roundedPlan` struck its arcs from the
literals 2.5/6.5 while `planIsLegal` computes its centres AS `[R, R]`, so the two
disagreed the instant anyone touched the radius. Both now computed.

## 3. Traps already paid for — keyed by what you will SEE

| the picture / the symptom | the cause |
|---|---|
| the contact sheet of "the most viable blocks" is sixteen slabs wearing a small block as a hat | `ways` counted walking over the roof. A horizontal way must be **roofed**; a shaft is exempt, that is what makes it a shaft. |
| an L-of-walls has a hole where the two walls meet | overlapping polygons; parity reads the overlap as void |
| a role asking for ten barrel vaults returns **one** | the diversity key was the plan sequence and an arch has no plans — all 58 shared the empty string |
| sixteen recipes come back as forty-eight "unbuildable" fragments | `--recipes` split on commas, which are inside every recipe. Whitespace or semicolons. |
| a 10×10 contact sheet loses two rows off the bottom; standing back makes it a postage stamp | `blockshot` guessed its distance. It now projects the eight bbox corners and bisects. |
| `legality: BROKEN for rounded` from a plan whose source did not change | hardcoded arc centres vs centres derived from R |
| the tiler fills a plot perfectly and builds a **bunker** | exact-seam matching is biased to the commonest wall pattern, which is the solid wall. `--air` leans it toward rooms. |
| `census --check` / `kit --check` fail right after adding a plan | **expected.** That is the archive doing its job — read the diff. |
| a repair pass reports success and leaves a block ungrounded | a hard "only drop a surplus block" rule deadlocks when one role holds the whole kit. The quota is a preference. |

## 4. The owner's spec, and what it found

He coded his block set himself: **four sides clockwise from twelve, each in
three segments, X solid and O open.** 14 archetypes, 41 counting rotations.

**Only 6 of 13 existed** — and the gap was the simplest piece in architecture:

```
sides that are a complete wall:
  0 walls : bar, bar-wide, corner, tee, cross, rounded, drum, quarters, bore
  1 wall  : -- NOTHING --
  2 walls : ell, ell-deep, twin
  3 walls : notch
  4 walls : full, frame, shaft
```

`twin` is literally two walls side by side; nobody ever wrote the single. Every
configuration starting from one wall was unreachable in consequence.

Eight plans added — `wall`, `wall-ell`, `wall-tee`, `wall-curve`,
`corners-two`, `corner-small`, `corners-side`, `stub` — taking it to **13 of
14**. Free by the grammar's own contract: no existing recipe names them, so
nothing already built changed.

### The one that is still open: his #28, and a handedness slip

His codes are not consistently handed, and it cost a mis-parse. Measured both
readings against the real geometry:

| case | his code | walking CW | walking CCW |
|---|---|---|---|
| #4 one wall | `XXX XOO OOO OOX` | **matches** | — |
| #24 one corner | `OOO OOO XOO OOX` | — | **matches** |
| #28 two corners | `XOO OOO XOO XOX` | — | — |

4 reads clockwise, 24 anticlockwise. **28 satisfies neither**: `W = XOX` is right
either way — both ends of that side filled — but N and S cannot *both* read
`XOO`, because the two corners sit at opposite ends of the block, so whichever
way you walk round, one is a first segment and the other is a third.
`XOO/OOX`, never `XOO/XOO`. **The shape is unambiguous and is built
(`corners-side`); only the written code is mirrored.**

## 5. The interface is a QUERY, not a label

`js/aperture.js` implements his notation — sides clockwise from twelve, a letter
for the opening type, a digit for the floor, `-` for solid:

```
D1, D1, -, B2      S:ell,notch/3,shaft:stone
```

What makes it work is his rule: **for walkable space you get one opening per
level.** Two on one side at different levels is legal and joined by stairs, but
exceptional. Applying it, the walkable core is well spread — `+` 303, `T` 99,
`L` 72, `I` 39, dead-end 27, sealed 27 — against **91% `+`** if you count any
opening on any floor, which is porosity, not walkable space.

**And it queries.** Asked for his worked example — north solid, east on floor 2,
south and west on floor 1 — the grammar returned **six blocks that are exactly
that junction**. So "hand-picked variants that meet all those needs" is a lookup
over the enumeration, and a junction with **zero** hits is a precise statement of
which plan is missing.

Letters A–H are his, verbatim (`A` open, `B` solid, `C` 2+2, `D` 3+3, `E/F` the
2-yard bar, `G/H` the 3-yard). **Past H they are provisional and marked so in
the source** — only `TYPES` needs changing.

## 6. Decisions parked, awaiting the owner

### 6.1 R = 2 and the simplified ladder — ✅ APPLIED, and the prediction scored

**The owner settled it the same day**: *"we got rid of the 2.5 corner and reduced
it down to a radius of 2."* `R = 2`, `PLANES = [0, 2, 3, 4.5, 6, 7, 9]`. The
analysis below was written before it was applied and is left as it stood, so it
can be scored. It was right about the ladder and wrong about the cure.

| pre-registered | measured after |
|---|---|
| dead ends 1 → **0** | **0.** The all-`rounded` block met nothing in 66,920; it now meets 8,000-odd. |
| sealed chambers 88 → **134** | **134**, to the block. |
| sole-source words 4 → 3 | **3**, but not the predicted three: `rounded`→`chamfer` vanished entirely, and `ell-deep` split into a handed pair. |
| worst reach penalty 8.1× → 2.0× *with `twin(2)`* | **1.16×, WITHOUT it.** |
| `twin(2)` is the cure | **NOT NEEDED.** |

**Two things the prediction could not have known.**

`twin(2)` was proposed because at R = 2 the corner arcs leave a gap and
`quarters` starts speaking `##.....##`, which nothing else made *at the time
that was measured*. The wall family shipped in between, and **`wall-tee` speaks
exactly that word**. The disease was cured by work done for another reason
before the medicine was ever mixed. A pre-registered fix has to be re-measured
against the tree it is finally applied to.

And the sole-source penalty did not merely shrink, it **inverted**: a block
carrying a sole-source word now has a *higher* floor than one without (worst
reach 6 against 0.25). Whatever makes a block hard to place at R = 2, it is no
longer its rarest word. That is a new open question, not a closed one.

**What it cost:** 17 edge words instead of 15 and 2,786 triples instead of
1,764 — a richer vocabulary — so the hundred-block kit dropped from 96/100
fully flush to 82/100. Still one component, still 0 orphans, still tiles a
5×5×3 with every seam exact. The kit spec has not been re-tuned for the wider
vocabulary and probably should be.

**And it is what made the drawing board's circles work.** At 2.5 a corner arc
crossed its edges between planes; at 2 it crosses ON them, the corner cell is
exactly R × R, and a corner round became a property of one cell. See §8.

The original analysis, unedited:

**2.5 is not merely hard to tell from 3 — it is the same word.** `ell(2.5)` and
`ell(3)` both emit `###......`; `ell(6.5)` and `ell(7)` both emit `#######..`.
At block resolution the half-yard planes express nothing their whole-yard
neighbours do not, so **dropping 2.5 and 6.5 costs exactly zero vocabulary.**
That settles the ladder simplification independently of R.

On the radius itself:

| | 2.5 shipped | 2 alone | 3 | **2 + twin(2)** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| sole-source words | 4 | 4 | 4 | **3** |
| dead ends | 1 | 1 | 1 | **0** |
| worst reach penalty | 8.1× | 8.7× | 8.1× | **2.0×** |
| baseline reach | 239 | 302 | 239 | **328** |
| column grown by four blocks | 15 ft | 12 ft | 18 ft | **12 ft** |

R=2 alone just moves the disease: at 2.5 the corner arcs overlap so `quarters`
speaks `door` (four makers); at 2 they leave a gap and it starts speaking
`##.....##`, which nothing else makes. **`twin(2)` is the cure** — it speaks
exactly that word — and it is also the owner's letter **C**, so it is wanted
twice over.

Costs: sealed chambers 88 → 134, and everything regenerates.

### 6.2 Still not built

- **The springer.** No block presents a spandrel on one face and a wall on the
  other, so **a vault can run forever or stop in mid-air and nothing else.** An
  arch's cap is met by 4 blocks and all 4 are arches. This is a new PLAN, not a
  kit slot, and it is the top structural gap.
- **The chains.** The owner's original ask — viable anchors "should connect to
  other anchor points on other blocks". Rings exist and are what a chain
  shackles to; nothing spans between them, and a chain swags, so it is a
  catenary and not a segment. The census says which blocks carry anchors.
- **Archetypes 24/28 handedness** — see §4.
- **The game.** He raised 3-D dominoes / Waterworks, and the numbers are
  encouraging: the difficulty is one cliff, between **1 neighbour** (36 of 400
  pieces fit, stuck 1% of the time) and **2** (median 0, stuck 58%). Sprawl is
  free, **closing a loop is the achievement**, and the 27 dead-end and 27 sealed
  blocks are the sabotage cards. The constraint has to come from a bounded plot,
  not the hand. `tools/assemble.mjs` is already the solitaire player.

## 8. The drawing board — `draw.html`, and the `D:` family

*"can you make a block builder so i can design specific blocks? something with
3 layers, these slices, and a ramp tool."*

`js/drawn.js` is the grammar and the geometry; `js/draw.js` is the shell. Read
`drawn.js` first — the header states the four things that make a hand-drawn
block safe rather than a hole in the cube law.

```
D:<layer>,<layer>,<layer>:<material>
layer  = <rectangles>[~<corners>][*<disc>][!<ramps>]   or  -
```

Half-yards, base 36: nine yards is eighteen is `i`, so a coordinate is one
character, a rectangle four and a ramp five. **Yards, never cell indices** —
indices are positions in a list `PLANES` regenerates, which is the exact bug
`recipe.js` exists to kill.

| | |
|---|---|
| the board | cells are the gaps between adjacent `PLANES`. Every edge lands on a plane by construction; there is nowhere illegal to put the pen. |
| the partition | a painted region becomes DISJOINT maximal rectangles, never a union. §2.3 — the overlap-reads-as-void trap is unreachable from the board, and `decode` refuses it anyway for recipes that arrive from elsewhere. |
| the ramp | one storey of rise over ≥ one storey of run, so 45° is the steepest. Written once in an (a,b,z) frame; the four directions are four right-handed placements of it. |
| the curves | `~occo` — one character per corner, `o` a column and `c` a cove. `*d`/`*s`/`*b` — a drum, or the storey bored at R or at the whole block. |
| one occupant per cell | fill, ramp, round and drum all stamp the same mask, because in the end they are all the same refusal. |

**`extrudePlan` lives in `plan.js` and `DECKS` in `cube.js`** so both composers
raise stone by the same act. That is what makes a drawn `full` and
`S:full,full,full` the *same block* rather than lookalikes — and the test that
says so now covers **every plan in the vocabulary**, curves included.

**Traps, keyed by what you will see:**

| the symptom | the cause |
|---|---|
| an old drawing comes back "corner off the slice planes" | it was drawn when 2.5 was a plane. Report, never substitute — the board falls back and the game warns. |
| a round refuses everywhere but four cells | correct. Only the corner cell is R × R; the arcs fit nowhere else. |
| the bucket floods the whole storey | correct. It is a bucket, and an empty storey is one region. |
| a bore refuses to share its layer | correct, and it is what boring through something means. |

`serve.mjs --shots` (`npm run serve:shots`) is what made any of this
verifiable: the live page POSTs a canvas to `docs/shots/live/`, which is the
only way to *see* what the board is drawing when the preview pane is not
displayed and screenshots time out. It duplicates `tools/snap.mjs`, found
afterwards — BACKLOG 0n retires that.

Everything about the *drawing* — cast shadows, staffage, the halo, ornament,
the renderer refinements — is untouched by all of this and still ranked in
`docs/BACKLOG.md`.
