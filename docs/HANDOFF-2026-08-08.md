`docs/BACKLOG.md` has the detail on each. Two things are worth carrying out of
it whatever you work on next.

**0w respected its own trap.** The morning handoff warned that rewriting saved
anchor keys to geometry is exact *only while the sampler agrees with the one
that wrote them*, and that a migration which silently walks every torch is
worse than an index that fails visibly. So `stack.js samplerStamp()` hashes the
sampler's actual output over eight probes and `SAMPLER` pins it. Change
`anchorsFor` and a test fails with a note saying what it means; `reindex` then
refuses and old keys stay exactly where they are. **Do not just re-pin the
constant** — that is the silent migration, wearing the fix as a disguise.

**0p was measured before it was fixed.** The obvious reading — 17 edge words
had outgrown a hundred blocks — was wrong. 15 of the 18 short blocks had a
replacement satisfying the *same role filter* that was flush on all four, and
the unmet words were not rare: one had 1,218 partners in the grammar. The
quotas were innocent; the greedy pass was the problem, and `docs/kit-spec.json`
now carries a `reviewed` note saying so, so nobody re-argues a settled question.

## THE PRIORITIES — what is next

### 1. A springer  ·  BACKLOG 0f

**The oldest open item, and it is now standing in front of two other things.**
Nothing in the grammar presents a spandrel on one face and a wall on the other,
so a vault runs forever or stops in mid-air. That was already the top of the
list; what is new is that it has started showing up in unrelated measurements.
Of the ten kit blocks still short of a wall, **eight have no same-role
candidate anywhere in 66,920 — and four of those are arches**. The kit's
interlock number and the vault's dead end are the same fact counted twice.

It is a new PLAN, not a kit slot and not a new block. `halfVault` exists and
solves the problem along the wrong axis: its hand picks which *spandrel*
survives, where what is wanted is a piece that is arch on one side and masonry
on the other.

### 2. Anchors have no chains  ·  the owner's own spec, unbuilt

> "if you do have viable anchor points they should connect to other anchor
> points on other blocks, perhaps with ropes or hanging chains."

Half of that is built — sites, viability, the picker, three kinds, and now keys
that survive a sampler change. The *connecting* half is not, and it is the half
he described first. `BACKLOG §3` has the shape: a catenary primitive, because
chains swag, and free placement that ignores the lattice.

### 3. The board cannot draw an arch  ·  BACKLOG 0l

`A:` springs at 4.5 and crowns on the boundary, which is a vertical freedom the
three-storey extrusion has not got. It is the one thing you can build with that
you cannot draw, and it is the same missing piece as 1 — the springer is what
would let a drawn block and a vault meet at all.

Below those: `0m` a ramp's toe is a knife edge with nothing said about what
holds it up; `0n` retire `tools/snap.mjs`; `0q` the sole-source penalty
inverted at R = 2 and nobody knows what now sets the floor on placeability;
`0g` `rounded` can only meet itself; `0h` the 88 sealed chambers.

## The one lesson to carry

Last time it was *write the test as the whole sequence a player performs*. That
held — the two-tab test caught a token keyed to the tab instead of to the write,
which refused about half the time and looked like it worked. But **six**
adversarial passes over this round's own work found twenty-two more, and six of
those were made by the fixes themselves. The shape repeats:

> `state.conflict` is set for a good reason and nothing ever clears it, so the
> autosave refuses for the rest of the session. Three separate doors led there:
> another tab deleting the building, this tab deleting it, and a `save` that
> returned before draining.

**A fix that adds a refusal adds a way to lose work, and the new way is always
in the paths that were safe before it.** Saving could not fail, so nothing
guarded `new`, `clear`, `import`, the buildings list, or closing the tab —
five doors that were correct right up until the moment the first `return` went
into `save()`. When you add a guard, list every caller that assumed the thing
you are guarding always succeeded, and walk each one.

The other half of it: **a derived quantity written down beside the thing it
derives from is a bug with a delay on it, and the delay can be under an hour.**
`naming.js` opens with that rule. I stored a content hash in the record the same
morning, widened the hash four commits later, and left every stored value lying.
It is regenerated on read now, like every other name in this project.

**And on how many passes are enough.** Each pass used a DIFFERENT LENS and the
count fell 10, 4, 4, 1, 4, 0: work-losing control flow, then a cold read, then a
cold read of the biggest file alone, then performance at scale (which found a
number, not a bug, and the answer was to measure and leave it), then hostile
data, then a full local CI run. The fifth lens found four things after the third
had found nothing new of its kind — **a pass that finds nothing means that lens
is exhausted, not that the code is clean.** Change the lens before you stop.
